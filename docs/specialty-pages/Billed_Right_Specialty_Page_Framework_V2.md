# Billed Right Specialty Page Framework — V2
## Master Implementation Standard for Raul, Marketing, Content & Web Development

**Version:** 2.0  
**Updated:** September 14, 2026  
**Purpose:** Standardize all Billed Right specialty pages around specialty expertise, enterprise positioning, search/AI visibility, and qualified lead conversion.

---

# 1. Core Objective

Every specialty page must quickly answer:

1. **Do you understand my specialty?**
2. **Do you understand where our revenue is leaking?**
3. **How will Billed Right improve it?**
4. **Can you support an organization of our size and complexity?**
5. **What evidence do you have?**
6. **What should I do next?**

The page framework stays consistent. The expertise and content must change by specialty.

### Specialty-Specific Content Standard

Approximately **60–70% of the meaningful content on each page should be genuinely specialty-specific.**

Do not create specialty pages by copying another page and replacing the specialty name.

---

# 2. The First Five Seconds Matter

The top of the page is the most important conversion area.

A visitor should understand within approximately five seconds:

- This page is specifically for their specialty.
- Billed Right understands their revenue-cycle problems.
- Billed Right focuses on financial outcomes, not simply billing activity.
- There is credible evidence that Billed Right can help.
- There is an obvious next action.

Reduce generic marketing language above the fold.

---

# 3. Standard Specialty Page Flow

Use this as the default sequence:

1. **Hero + Immediate Proof**
2. **Why [Specialty] Revenue Cycle Is Different**
3. **Top Revenue Leaks / Pain Areas**
4. **How Billed Right Solves Them**
5. **Common [Specialty] Denials**
6. **Specialty EMRs & Technology**
7. **What to Expect / Verified KPIs**
8. **Data to Decisions / Executive Visibility**
9. **Built for Growth & Scale**
10. **Specialty Case Study / Testimonial**
11. **Helpful Resources**
12. **Specialty FAQs**
13. **Final CTA**

The exact visual design may vary, but the buyer journey should remain logical:

**Attention → Relevance → Pain → Solution → Expertise → Evidence → Trust → Conversion**

# 4. Section-by-Section Requirements

## Section 1 — Hero + Immediate Proof

### H1 Structure

`[Specialty] Revenue Cycle Management Services`

The supporting copy should immediately connect Billed Right to specialty-specific financial outcomes.

Potential outcome themes:

- reduce preventable denials
- improve collections
- accelerate reimbursement
- improve A/R performance
- increase revenue visibility
- create more predictable cash flow
- support growth without losing revenue-cycle control

### Proof Above the Fold

Where verified information is available, include one or more concise proof elements near the hero:

- years of experience
- relevant client/provider scale
- verified collection or performance metric
- specialty experience
- relevant EMR expertise

**Do not use unverified numbers simply because they improve the design.**

### Primary CTA

**Schedule a Revenue Cycle Assessment**

### Secondary CTA

**Talk to an RCM Expert**

Mobile must include an easy one-click call option.

---

## Section 2 — Why [Specialty] Revenue Cycle Is Different

Demonstrate specialty understanding before selling services.

Research and explain approximately 4–6 realities that make the specialty different.

Examples:

- payer behavior
- reimbursement complexity
- specialty procedures
- recurring treatment models
- documentation dependencies
- authorization requirements
- coding complexity
- provider types
- site-of-service differences
- specialty workflow
- treatment frequency
- specialty technology

### Quality Test

A knowledgeable practice executive should think:

> “They understand how our specialty actually operates.”

---

## Section 3 — Top Revenue Leaks / Pain Areas

This should be one of the strongest sections.

Identify approximately **3–6 major revenue leaks** for the specialty.

For each:

**Revenue Leak → Why It Happens → Financial/Operational Impact**

Examples could include:

- preventable denials
- missed/incorrect charges
- payer-specific issues
- eligibility problems
- authorization breakdowns
- coding/documentation issues
- underpayments
- aging A/R
- workflow gaps
- credentialing/enrollment issues

### Important

A pain point can be discussed even when it is **not included in Billed Right's standard RCM scope**.

However, do not imply that Billed Right's core RCM service performs a function that it does not.

---

## Section 4 — How Billed Right Solves the Revenue Problems

Connect the pain areas directly to Billed Right's actual capabilities.

Think in terms of:

**Problem → Billed Right Action → Expected Business Impact**

Core themes may include:

- revenue-cycle workflow discipline
- claim quality
- rejection management
- denial prevention and resolution
- payment posting
- A/R follow-up
- payer follow-up
- revenue leakage identification
- analytics
- performance monitoring
- process improvement
- automation and technology

### Critical Service-Scope Rule

**Do not automatically describe coding or prior authorization as included core RCM services.**

AI-generated content frequently makes this mistake.

Where Billed Right offers coding, prior authorization, credentialing, or other holistic services separately, clearly identify them as:

**Additional / Add-On / Holistic Services**, as appropriate.

Never blur core RCM scope and optional services.

---

## Section 5 — Common [Specialty] Denials

### Suggested Heading

**Common [Specialty] Denials We Help Address and Prevent**

Use a simple structure:

| Denial / Revenue Issue | Common Root Cause | Billed Right Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Specialty-specific issue | Why it occurs | Prevention, monitoring or resolution approach |

Focus on the causes of denials, not merely downstream follow-up.

This section should reinforce Billed Right's Intelligent RCM positioning:

**Identify → Prevent → Resolve → Learn → Improve**

Do not imply that Billed Right performs authorization or coding work unless that service is actually part of the client's contracted scope.

---

## Section 6 — Specialty EMRs & Technology

This section is mandatory.

### Suggested Heading

**Experience With the Technology [Specialty] Organizations Use**

Do not simply repeat the generic company-wide EMR list.

Research and display the systems most relevant to that specialty and prioritize systems based on Billed Right's actual experience.

Examples across Billed Right may include:

- eClinicalWorks
- athenahealth
- IMS
- AdvancedMD
- CentralReach
- other verified platforms

### Specialty Example

An ABA page should emphasize ABA-relevant platforms such as CentralReach when supported by Billed Right experience.

A Cardiology page may prioritize eClinicalWorks, athenahealth and other systems based on actual Billed Right client experience.

### Positioning

Billed Right works within the client's technology environment while adding:

- operational discipline
- revenue-cycle intelligence
- analytics
- automation
- performance visibility

---

# 5. Specialty Segmentation Rules

Do not force materially different buyer groups into one generic specialty page.

If a segment has substantially different:

- terminology
- workflows
- payer requirements
- CPT/service mix
- technology
- pain points
- buyer search behavior

consider creating a dedicated page.

### Example: Behavioral Health vs. ABA

ABA should be evaluated as its own specialty page because its workflows, terminology, EMRs, payer requirements and search behavior can differ materially from general Psychiatry/Behavioral Health.

Do not overstate Billed Right's market leadership or experience in a specialty where evidence is limited.

We can demonstrate knowledge without claiming to be the industry's leading provider.

# 7. Data to Decisions / Executive Visibility

This is a major Billed Right differentiator.

Do not position reporting as simply “monthly billing reports.”

Show how revenue-cycle information helps leadership understand:

- where revenue is being lost
- why it is happening
- which payer/provider/location/service line is affected
- what action is being taken
- whether performance is improving

Depending on the specialty, relevant visibility may include:

- collections trends
- A/R aging
- denial trends
- payer performance
- provider performance
- location performance
- reimbursement trends
- rejection trends

- operational exceptions

The section should be meaningful to:

**CEO | CFO | COO | Practice Executive | Administrator | Revenue Cycle Leader**

# 9. Case Studies & Testimonials

## Case Studies

Whenever possible, use a case study related to the same specialty.

Do not force an unrelated case study into a specialty page merely because one is available.

If no specialty case study exists:

- use an appropriate verified company proof point, or
- create a Marketing action item to develop the case study.

Never invent a case study.

## Testimonials

Avoid generic anonymous testimonials.

Where client approval allows, provide authentic context such as:

**First Name / Dr. First Name — Specialty — City/State**

Additional organizational context can be included when approved.

Testimonials must be real and approved.

Do not rewrite authentic client language into exaggerated AI-sounding marketing copy.

# 13. SEO, AEO & AI Discoverability

Each page must be useful to people first, while also being technically understandable to search and AI systems.

## Required Content Elements

- one clear H1
- logical H2/H3 hierarchy
- primary specialty terminology
- semantic variations
- specialty-specific pain points
- specialty-specific denial terminology
- relevant service terminology
- relevant CPT/HCPCS context
- payer/authorization terminology where useful
- specialty EMRs
- useful FAQs
- internal links
- relevant resources
- title/meta description
- appropriate schema/structured data where technically valid

## AI Search Principles

Create content that systems such as Google AI, ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and other answer engines can clearly interpret.

Prefer:

- factual statements
- descriptive headings
- concise definitions
- problem/cause/solution structures
- clear tables
- specialty terminology
- meaningful FAQs
- original Billed Right evidence

Avoid:

- keyword stuffing
- generic AI filler
- repetitive marketing language
- unsupported superlatives

The goal is to create content worth **finding, understanding, citing and recommending**.

# 15. Images & Visual Content

Specialty pages should not be large blocks of text.

Use relevant, professional visual assets to improve engagement.

Priority:

1. authentic Billed Right/client-approved imagery where available
2. existing licensed company image libraries
3. existing company subscriptions such as Envato or other approved sources
4. newly licensed/generated visual assets where appropriate

Raul/Marketing should consolidate company-owned/licensed image resources rather than relying on personal accounts long term.

### Image Rules

- images should fit the specialty
- avoid obviously generic medical stock photography where possible
- optimize images for page speed
- use descriptive filenames
- use accurate alt text
- never imply that a stock-image physician or facility is a Billed Right client

- Hero CTA
- contextual CTA after meaningful problem/solution content
- final CTA

### Primary CTA

**Schedule a Revenue Cycle Assessment**

- Talk to an RCM Expert
- Phone
- Contact form

# 17. Lead Form Requirements

Keep forms simple.

Do not request information merely because it may be useful later.

Initial lead form should generally focus on information needed to contact the prospect, such as:

- Name
- Work Email
- Phone
- Practice / Organization Name

Avoid unnecessary specialty dropdowns and excessive qualification questions when the visitor is already on a specialty page.

The page URL/source can provide specialty context to the CRM.

Review wording such as **“Free Billing Review”** and use language consistent with the approved revenue-cycle assessment positioning.

---

# 18. CRM & Lead Response

Website conversion is incomplete until Sales knows the lead exists.

Every website lead should:

1. enter Zoho CRM correctly
2. preserve source/page information
3. trigger rapid notification to the appropriate Sales/Marketing team
4. have clear ownership
5. support quick follow-up

Marketing/IT should ensure the workflow alerts the appropriate people, potentially through Teams/email/CRM notification.

### Principle

**Speed-to-lead matters.**

A strong website cannot compensate for delayed response after a qualified prospect submits a request.

---

# 19. Live Chat Rule

Live chat should only be promoted if Billed Right can respond quickly.

Do not create the expectation of a real-time conversation if the team must keep a browser page open to notice the message.

Before relying on live chat, confirm:

- mobile/app notifications
- Sales ownership
- availability expectations
- after-hours behavior
- escalation
- CRM integration

A third-party solution may be appropriate if it creates reliable mobile notifications and rapid response.

Until the operating model supports live response, do not let chat become a negative prospect experience.

# 23. Pre-Publish Quality Checklist

Do not publish until Marketing can answer **YES** to the important items below.

### Specialty Relevance

- Does the hero capture attention immediately?
- Is the page clearly written for this specialty?
- Is approximately 60–70% of meaningful content specialty-specific?
- Are the specialty's major revenue leaks addressed?
- Are denials specialty-specific?
- Are relevant specialty EMRs included?

### Accuracy

- Are all Billed Right service claims accurate?
- Are coding and authorization clearly distinguished from core RCM where appropriate?
- Are all KPIs internally verified?
- Are case studies real?
- Are testimonials real and approved?
- Are all certifications/credentials accurate?
- Are CPT/HCPCS references validated and contextual?

### Enterprise Buyer

- Would this page be useful to a CEO/CFO/COO/administrator/RCM leader?
- Does it demonstrate scalability?
- Does it demonstrate decision-quality data?
- Does it focus on business outcomes?

### Search / AI

- Is there one clear H1?
- Are headings logical?
- Is specialty terminology natural and complete?
- Are FAQs genuinely useful?
- Can search engines understand the page?
- Can AI answer engines understand Billed Right's specialty expertise?

### Conversion

- Is there a strong hero CTA?
- Is the form simple?
- Is one-click calling available on mobile?
- Does the form create the correct CRM lead?
- Is specialty/source attribution preserved?
- Are Sales notifications working?
- Has the complete lead workflow been tested?
- If chat is enabled, can someone respond quickly?
