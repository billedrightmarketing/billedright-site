# 2. WHAT MAKES INTERNAL MEDICINE FINANCIALLY DIFFERENT

Do not simply copy the Cardiology or Pain Management story.

Internal Medicine frequently combines:

- high recurring patient volume
- patients with multiple chronic conditions
- preventive and longitudinal care
- managed-care/capitation arrangements
- fee-for-service reimbursement
- payer-specific requirements
- diagnosis/documentation dependencies
- performance and incentive programs
- patient financial responsibility
- provider-level variation
- multi-provider and multi-location operations

For managed-care-heavy practices, large A/R balances may not be the central financial problem.

The more important questions can be:

- Is the patient correctly identified under the applicable managed-care/capitated or fee-for-service pathway?
- Is coverage information accurate?
- Are the diagnoses supported by the encounter being captured appropriately?
- Are recurring diagnosis/documentation gaps visible?
- Are providers being educated when recurring issues or coding changes are identified?
- What do quarterly performance reports show?
- Where is the practice falling short against applicable program requirements?
- What financial or incentive opportunities require attention?
- For fee-for-service revenue, where are claims, denials, payments or patient balances getting stuck?

The page should demonstrate that Billed Right understands **how the practice is paid**, not merely how to submit a claim.
